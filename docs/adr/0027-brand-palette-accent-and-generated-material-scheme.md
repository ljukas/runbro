# 27. Brand palette: an iOS accent from the asset catalog, a generated Material scheme on Android, and segment colours rebuilt around the brand

Date: 2026-10-17

## Status

Accepted (2026-10-17, on merge of #101). It reverses [ADR 0025](0025-android-staged-migration.md) §4
("Android colour is dynamic"); ADR 0025 carries an amendment pointing here. It
changes the token values of [ADR 0002](0002-uniwind-styling-engine.md), but not
the mechanism.

**Revision 2.** Revision 1 went through three adversarial reviews on 2026-10-17:
evidence (every number re-derived), feasibility against the codebase, and
premises. This revision applies their findings:

- the brand hue's meaning is scoped to data marks (§1, §5);
- increased contrast gets a real mechanism (§2);
- the OTA claim is corrected (§3, Consequences);
- every Android wallpaper reader is moved, not only the one theme file (§4);
- the scheme variant changes to `SchemeContent` (§4);
- the colour-vision rule covers every pair drawn together, measured as ΔE00 (§5).

**Revision 3.** A re-review of revision 2 left one Major finding: one shared JSON
required by the config plugin would have put every colour into both native
fingerprints. Revision 3 applies it:

- the values split into an iOS-accent file and a JS-only file (§3);
- four more failing pairs are named (§5);
- the header gap is stated (§2).

## Context

The app has a brand now: the **Dash R** on a sunrise gradient, amber `#FFB224` →
coral `#FF4256`, mid `#FF7A3D`. The app's colours ignore it:

- iOS uses a blue primary (`#3c87f7` light, `#5C9DFF` dark) and system blue
  (`#007AFF`) for the run segments.
- Android uses the wallpaper.
- Warm-up is already orange, and the running stat is system red.

The store screenshots wait for the app to match its icon, and the icon, the splash
and this palette ship in one native build.

What constrains the decision (the research and spike sections have the sources):

- **The brand fails contrast in light mode.** On white, amber is 1.80:1, mid 2.59:1
  and coral 3.41:1. In dark mode `#FF7A3D` passes as text (6.57:1 on `#1C1C1E`), but
  a white label on it doesn't (2.59:1).
- **Apple's HIG (Branding, 2026-09-09):**
  - "Apply your app's accent color judiciously."
  - "**Minimize its use on controls** and instead use it intentionally for primary
    actions or status indicators … or an icon for the selected tab."
  - "Avoid using the same color to mean different things" (Color).
  - Supply light, dark and increased-contrast variants.
  - The Color page also says a brand accent "can be an effective way to tailor"
    an app whose content is mostly monochrome. That describes ours: neutral cards
    and lists, with colour in the run data.
- **Spike 1:**
  - An asset-catalog `AccentColor` reaches the iOS tab bar, SwiftUI `List` buttons
    and untinted buttons, and its variants switch live.
  - It doesn't reach `Toggle`s, alerts, or anything tinted explicitly.
  - It doesn't reach **native-stack headers either.** expo-router hands them
    `theme.colors.primary` (system blue from `DefaultTheme`) as an explicit tint
    (`useHeaderConfigProps.js:114`).
  - Expo SDK 57 has no app-config key for the accent.
- **Spike 2 and the review's re-derivation:**
  - `@expo/ui` 57.0.20 seeds Compose only with `SchemeTonalSpot`. In light mode its
    primary is brown (`#8E4D30`), and in both modes its container and tertiary
    roles differ from a brand-faithful scheme.
  - `SchemeFidelity` and `SchemeContent` keep a burnt-orange primary (`#A53C00`)
    and use the seed itself as `primaryContainer`. Their tertiary differs:
    Fidelity's is cyan (`#00B0CC` container), which would collide with a teal
    segment, and Content's is olive (`#B2A300`).
  - The library runs on Hermes (+216 KB) and leaves the native fingerprint
    unchanged.
- **The owner wants Android "as close as we can to Material Design"** and has
  chosen the brand over wallpaper colour. That is a deliberate departure from
  Material's default on Android 12+, which is dynamic colour first, with brand
  colours added individually and harmonized. What remains Material is the
  machinery: a full Material 3 role scheme generated by Google's own algorithm,
  the way Theme Builder's content-based "color match" does it.

## Decision

### 1. One brand hue, with shades by role and by scheme

All shades share the mid's HCT hue (about 41°). They keep chroma 64.5 wherever the
gamut allows; the pale tone-80 shades drop to chroma about 30.

| Token | Light | Dark | Light, increased contrast | Dark, increased contrast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `primary` (tint, links) | `#BC4A0C` | `#FF7A3D` | `#A53C00` | `#FFB598` |
| `primary-foreground` (label on `primary`) | white (5.09:1) | black (8.10:1) | white | black |
| `primary-fill` (filled controls) | `#BC4A0C` | `#C75115` | `#A53C00` | `#FFB598` |
| `primary-fill-foreground` | white (5.09:1) | white (4.55:1) | white (6.47:1) | black (12.3:1) |

- **One shade per scheme where possible.** Light `primary` and `primary-fill` are
  the same shade: the two candidates were only ΔE76 3.3 apart.
- **Dark `primary-fill` is darker than the tint**, because a white label on the
  real `#FF7A3D` fails.
- **In increased-contrast dark, the fill flips to a pale fill with a black label,**
  so contrast goes up against the card instead of down.
- **The hex values are pinned, not regenerated.** Two of them sit near the
  threshold (`#BC4A0C` at 4.56:1 on the grouped gray, white on `#C75115` at
  4.55:1). A test checks that every pair above stays ≥ 4.5:1.
- **The gradient is decoration only.** It never carries text or a control, and it
  sits in the content layer, never on Liquid Glass chrome. The plan names each
  place it appears.

### 2. iOS: the accent in the asset catalog, read by name

- **An owned config plugin** (`plugins/with-brand-colors.js`, next to
  `with-health.js`) writes two colour sets: `AccentColor` (the `primary` column) and
  `PrimaryFill`. Each has all four variants.
  - It sets `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME` on the app target's
    configurations.
  - It resolves the source root per variant (`IOSConfig.Paths`), not a hard-coded
    `RunBro`.
  - It `require()`s its values from the iOS accent file (§3), so the fingerprint
    tracks them.
  - A future extension target (ADR 0022) brings its own colour set.
- **Explicit iOS tints read the named colours.** These are `Island.Button`,
  progress, the onboarding symbols and the selected-tab overrides. They use
  `PlatformColor('AccentColor' | 'PrimaryFill')`, not hex, so they get the
  increased-contrast variants. The plan verifies that `@expo/ui` and expo-symbols
  resolve named colours (expo-modules-core converts them via `UIColor(named:)`).
  - Where a named colour can't reach, the hex tokens apply in light and dark,
    **without an increased-contrast variant**. This is an accepted gap. It covers
    RN `Text` styled through Uniwind and native-stack headers, since
    `navigationTheme()` stays hex because some React Navigation paths call
    `Color()` on it.
- **`navigationTheme()` sets `colors.primary` to the accent hex per scheme,** so
  header back buttons follow it (expo-router reads it on iOS only).
  `headerTintColor` stays unset, because it would also tint the title. The plan
  verifies `Stack.Toolbar` buttons.
- **The accent is meant for the app's actions, in line with the HIG's "minimize on
  controls":** the selected tab, `List` buttons, the primary CTA, links. Toggles
  stay system green. Alerts stay system-styled. `success` and `destructive` stay
  system colours.

### 3. Two generated sources, split along the fingerprint

`@expo/fingerprint` hashes every module a config plugin loads, so one shared file
would make every segment or Android colour edit move both native fingerprints.
The values therefore live in two committed files, and one generator writes both:

- **`scripts/generate-brand-palette.ts`** uses `@material/material-color-utilities`
  as a **devDependency** and runs as `bun scripts/generate-brand-palette.ts`.
  - It has **no `package.json` script entry**, because that field is a fingerprint
    source on both platforms.
- **`plugins/brand-accent.json`** holds the §1 iOS shades (pinned).
  - The config plugin requires it, so it is a fingerprint source on purpose:
    changing an accent shade means a native build.
  - `theme.ts` imports it too. A JS import doesn't affect the fingerprint.
- **`src/constants/brand-palette.json`** holds everything else, and **JS imports it
  only**:
  - every Material 3 role of `SchemeContent` (seed `#FF7A3D`, contrast 0), light
    and dark;
  - the §5 segment and stat colours.
- **A `bun test` keeps them honest:**
  - `global.css` tokens equal the JSON (CSS can't import it);
  - §1's contrast floor;
  - §5's rule.

### 4. Android: the generated Content scheme in place of wallpaper colour

- **Every wallpaper read is replaced by the generated roles.** That is
  `material-theme.android.ts` (whose `getMaterialColors()` feeds
  `applyPlatformTheme()` and Uniwind) and every `useMaterialColors()` call in a
  Compose tree:
  - `onboarding-button`, `onboarding-carousel`, `onboarding-hero`,
    `permission-step-screen`, `plan-session-row`, `run-mode-card`.
  - A lint rule (`no-restricted-imports`) then bans `getMaterialColors` and
    `useMaterialColors`.
- **Compose controls take explicit colours** from the generated roles:
  - the button variants, `Switch`, `ListItem`, `Card` and `AlertDialog` take
    `colors`; `LinearProgressIndicator` takes `color`/`trackColor`;
  - this is set once in `island/` wrappers and the Android domain components
    (ADR 0013). `ListItem`, which screens use directly today, gets a wrapper.
- **`IslandHost` passes `seedColor="#FF7A3D"`.** Whatever we can't reach
  (ripples, focus, dialog internals, `OutlinedButton`'s border) sits on the brand
  hue as TonalSpot. Its container and tertiary roles differ from Content in both
  modes. This is an accepted, visible gap, closed by the upstream request below.
- **`NativeTabs` gets explicit Android colours** from the generated roles:
  - `backgroundColor`, `indicatorColor`, `rippleColor`, `tintColor` and the
    `labelStyle` colours;
  - `iconColor` too, gated to Android so it doesn't override the iOS accent.
- **The native window theme** takes the brand through `app.json` `primaryColor`
  (today `colorPrimary` is `#023c69`).
- **No "use wallpaper colours" setting.** Android 14+'s system contrast setting
  is also lost, because it acts only on dynamic schemes. Both are accepted.
- **Upstream:** file an Expo feature request for `Host` / `getMaterialColors` to
  take a full `ColorScheme` or a scheme variant. When it lands, the explicit
  `colors` go, along with the TonalSpot gap.

### 5. Orange means running in the data; segment and stat colours rebuilt

- **In data marks, the brand hue means running.** Data marks are the segment bar,
  legend, splits, the pace/elevation chart, the route track and the stat tiles.
  No other data mark uses that hue.
- **Elsewhere, orange is the accent:** actions and the selected tab. An accent-
  coloured element that isn't interactive doesn't sit on a surface that shows run
  data. Two today move to neutral:
  - the run summary headline's "not completed" (`text-primary` → `foreground`);
  - the map's start marker (`colors.primary`).
- **On Android, `primaryContainer` equals the dark run colour (`#FF7A3D`).** That
  is acceptable on cards and buttons, which aren't data marks; the scope above
  keeps it unambiguous.
- **The Material `tertiary` roles stay off data surfaces as well.** Content's olive
  `#B2A300` is ΔE00 3.1 from the run colour under deutan simulation. Today it sits
  only on the Run-tab chooser card.

| Segment | Light | Dark | Was |
|---|---|---|---|
| run | `#D95E23` | `#FF7A3D` | system blue |
| walk | `#8E8E93` | `#8E8E93` | (unchanged) |
| warm-up and cool-down | teal `#0A7C8C` | `#40C8E0` | orange / teal |
| stopped | indigo `#5856D6` | `#5E5CE6` | brown |

- **`StatColors.running` takes the run colour.**
- **These are starting values; the rule below is binding.** The review found
  these failures, so the plan must change at least one colour in each pair:
  - **the stop hairline (indigo) vs the pace line (purple)** on the chart: ΔE00
    about 4–6 under protan;
  - **the run strip vs the elevation line (green):** about 5;
  - **walk gray `#8E8E93` on the light session card** (`#F0F0F3`): 2.87:1;
  - **the `activeTime` blue vs `pace` purple** on the free-run stat grid: ΔE00
    4.4.
- **The rule.** For every pair of colours drawn on one surface (the segment bar,
  the chart with its strip, hairlines and lines, the route over Apple and Google
  map tiles, the stat tiles):
  - **3:1 against the card** for each graphic (WCAG 1.4.11);
  - **ΔE00 ≥ 10** between each pair under Machado 2009 protan/deutan/tritan
    (severity 1.0, applied in linear RGB);
  - **a mark thinner than 3 pt** (the stop hairline) also differs in form, such as
    a dash, so hue is never its only cue;
  - **run intervals keep the thicker stroke** (`ROUTE_STROKE_W_RUN`).

  Taken alone, the starting segment set passes the ΔE00 part: minimum 11.6 in
  light, 18.0 in dark.
- **Warm-up and cool-down share one colour,** because they are always first and
  last and are labelled. The legend shows one "Warm-up / cool-down" swatch. Warm-up
  shares walk's glyph, so warm-up vs walk rests on the segment set's tightest pair
  (walk vs teal). That pair passes, but the plan watches it.
- **The other stat accents are re-chosen under the same rule,** from Apple system
  colours where those pass. `distance` (indigo today) moves, because indigo now
  means stopped.

### 6. What does not change

- Token names, Uniwind, `@variant light/dark` and `applyPlatformTheme()` stay
  (ADR 0002).
- `ThemedText`/`ThemedView` and the ADR 0013 component rules stay.
- System fonts stay. Barlow is for marketing art only.
- The iOS neutrals, `success` and `destructive` stay.

## Consequences

- **Both apps match the icon.** iOS stays restrained (accent only); Android
  carries a full brand Material scheme.
- **Android loses Material You.** This is a deliberate departure from Material's
  default and a reversal of ADR 0025 §4, which now carries an amendment.
  - `AGENTS.md`'s "dynamic (wallpaper) colour" line and "Theming (Android)"
    paragraph change when this is built.
  - The stale comments in `island/host.android.tsx` and `material-theme.android.ts`
    go too.
- **Fingerprint and OTA (ADR 0012):**
  - The config plugin and its colour sets are native. Changing the **iOS accent
    shades** always needs a store build. The plugin's `require()` of
    `brand-accent.json` makes the fingerprint enforce that on both platforms, so an
    OTA can't desync JS tokens from the binary.
  - Segment, stat and Android colours live in the JS-only file and stay
    OTA-eligible.
  - The first build moves both fingerprints anyway: new icon and splash.
- **Accepted gaps:**
  - no increased contrast for Uniwind-styled RN text and native-stack headers on
    iOS;
  - TonalSpot defaults under Compose until Expo adds a scheme prop;
  - no Android system contrast;
  - no Material You.
- **Every run-colour surface changes at once.** They need emulator and simulator
  passes in both schemes, including the map over its tiles. Maestro is
  unaffected: flows select by text and assert no colours (ADR 0016).
- **Not decided here, but in the same build:** the splash background in dark mode
  and the adaptive icon background. They belong to the icon/splash roadmap items.
  The Android status-bar icon bug (white on light) is unrelated, and this palette
  doesn't fix it.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep wallpaper colour on Android.** This is the cheapest option and is
  Material's default. It was rejected because the Play listing would show an app
  that doesn't match its icon.
- **Brand by default with a "use wallpaper colours" setting** (the research's
  recommendation). It was rejected for now: it doubles the palettes to verify and
  needs the library at runtime to harmonize. It can be added later beside the
  generated scheme.
- **Brand colours added individually and harmonized into dynamic colour**
  (Material's own pattern). It was rejected for the same reason: the overall look
  stays the wallpaper's.
- **`SchemeFidelity`:** the same primary roles, but a cyan tertiary that collides
  with the warm-up/cool-down teal. Content was chosen instead.
- **`SchemeTonalSpot` through `IslandHost seedColor` alone.** It needs no work, but
  its light primary is a muted brown, not the brand.
- **Computing the scheme at runtime:** 216 KB and about 13 ms per launch for a seed
  that never changes. Generation was chosen instead.
- **Per-surface hex tints on iOS:** more call sites and no increased contrast.
  The asset accent plus named colours was chosen instead.
- **The bright gradient stops as interactive colours:** they fail 4.5:1 on white,
  and amber fails 3:1. They stay decorative.
- **Tinting iOS `Toggle`s with the brand:** the HIG allows it, but it was rejected
  under "minimize on controls".

## Amendment (2026-10-17): the values the plans pin

Written with the implementation plans. Every figure below came from a script that
reproduces each number in this ADR (the §1 contrasts, the §5 segment minimums
11.58 and 17.96, all four failing pairs) and CIEDE2000's published test pairs.
Stage 1 commits that maths as `src/domain/color-metrics.ts`. The 3:1 floor is now
measured on the Android cards too: the generated `SchemeContent` surfaces
(`surfaceContainerHigh`/`Highest`: `#FBE3DB`/`#F5DED6` light, `#342722`/`#40322C`
dark), not only the iOS ones.

**Three more starting values fail 3:1** on those surfaces:

- light run `#D95E23` is 2.92:1 on `#F5DED6`;
- light walk `#8E8E93` is 2.87:1 (already named in §5);
- dark stopped `#5E5CE6` is 2.43:1 on `#40322C`.

**The pinned data colours.** Each passes 3:1 on every card it sits on. Every pair
drawn on one surface stays ≥ ΔE00 10 under normal, protan, deutan and tritan
vision.

| | Light | Dark | Changed from §5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| run | `#C75115` | `#FF7A3D` | light, for contrast |
| walk | `#636366` | `#8E8E93` | light, for contrast |
| warm-up and cool-down | `#0A7C8C` | `#40C8E0` | no |
| stopped | `#5856D6` | `#7D7AFF` | dark, for contrast |
| pace (chart line, tile) | `#AF0F8A` | `#FF97D8` | purple → magenta (stop hairline pair) |
| elevation (chart line, tile) | `#008954` | `#00D385` | green re-picked (run strip pair) |

- **Minimums:** the segment set is 11.5 (light) and 17.8 (dark); the chart is
  12.2 and 13.2.
- **Hue:** every chromatic data colour sits ≥ 56° of HCT hue from run's 41°.
- **Light run vs accent:** light run is now close to the light accent (`#BC4A0C`,
  ΔE00 3.1). §5 scopes the hue's meaning by surface, so this is accepted.

**Three accents on the stat tiles, not five.** No five-colour set (Running, Active
Time, Distance, Pace, Elevation) passes the rule with Apple system colours in either
scheme. Custom sets pass only with dark, desaturated light-mode colours.

- **Coloured:** Running (the run colour), Pace and Elevation. Pace and Elevation
  match their chart lines.
- **Neutral:** Active Time, Distance and the free run's Moving Time. Moving Time
  includes walking, so it isn't running.
- `StatColors` keeps `running`, `pace` and `elevation` only.

**Coloured labels are text.** The tile labels and the profile card's unit captions
are 13 pt text, so WCAG 1.4.3 asks 4.5:1, not the 3:1 for graphics. Light run
(3.53:1) and elevation (3.46:1) miss it on the Android card.

- Labels and captions use `foreground-secondary`.
- The glyph, or a short line swatch on the profile card, keeps the colour at the
  graphic bar.

**The route gets a casing.** The tiles under a route can't be listed, and a running
route mostly lies on roads (dark-mode roads put walk and stopped near 2.6:1).

- Each route line draws over a casing 3 pt wider (1.5 pt a side) in the screen
  `background` colour.
- The rule measures route colours against the casing: ≥ 4.33:1 in both schemes.
- The start marker turns neutral (`foreground`); the finish keeps `success`.

**A third named colour, `PrimaryFillForeground`.** Increased-contrast dark flips the
fill label to black (§1), and SwiftUI draws a prominent button's label white unless
told otherwise. The config plugin writes this colour set too, and `Island.Button`
uses it for the label.

**`primary` as text stays off `background-element`.** Light `#BC4A0C` is 4.48:1 on
`#F0F0F3`. It clears 4.5:1 on `background`, `background-grouped` and
`background-card`, which is where the test measures it. No `text-primary` sits on an
element surface today.

**The gradient appears in one place:** the onboarding welcome hero.

- On iOS that is the app icon image the welcome screen already shows, so the new
  icon brings the gradient with it.
- On Android it is the welcome page's clover hero (stage 4).
- The run summary gets none, because it shows run data (§5).

**Measured on the simulator (stage 2).** iOS 26.5 and 17.5, light, dark and both increased-contrast states.

- The asset-catalog colours compile as specified. The tab bar, `List` buttons, the primary CTA (glass on 26, `borderedProminent` on 17.5, black label in increased-contrast dark), the progress bar, the onboarding symbols and the up-next arrow follow them.
- Named colours re-resolve live: a light/dark or contrast switch recolours symbols already on screen.
- On iOS 26, Liquid Glass bar buttons ignore the accent. The back button's glass pill and the `Stack.Toolbar` buttons (the summary's and the map's Close) draw in the label colour in every state. That is the system's monochrome toolbar style and agrees with "minimize its use on controls", so the app doesn't work around it. `navigationTheme()` keeps the accent hex for runtimes before iOS 26.
